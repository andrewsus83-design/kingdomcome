# St. Thérèse of Lisieux
**Category:** Saints
**Feast Day:** October 1
**Era:** 19th Century (1873–1897)
**Origin:** Alençon, Normandy, France
**Patronage:** Missions, France, florists, aviators, those suffering from serious illness, roses
**Rarity:** Epic

## Description
Thérèse Martin entered the Carmelite convent in Lisieux at age fifteen — the youngest novice ever accepted — and died of tuberculosis at just twenty-four, never leaving the cloister, never going on mission, never doing anything famous by the world's measurements. Yet she became one of the most beloved and influential saints in the entire history of the Church, declared a Doctor of the Faith alongside Thomas Aquinas and Teresa of Avila. Her secret was the "Little Way": the discovery that ordinary, tiny acts of love performed with complete devotion are just as holy and just as powerful as grand heroic deeds. For every young person who feels too small, too ordinary, or too overlooked to matter, Thérèse is the saint who says: every single thing you do with love is changing eternity.

## Character Attributes (Symbols)
- Deep red roses — she promised from heaven to "let fall a shower of roses" — graces and answered prayers — upon the earth; roses became her permanent symbol and miraculously appeared at her gravesite
- Wooden crucifix held to heart — she held this position constantly in her final illness, representing her complete union with Christ's suffering and her offering of every pain as a gift of love
- Brown Carmelite habit with white mantle — her hidden vocation of contemplative prayer, invisible to the world but transforming it from the inside; she called herself "a little brush in God's hand"
