# St. Catherine of Siena
**Category:** Saints
**Feast Day:** April 29
**Era:** 14th Century (1347–1380)
**Origin:** Siena, Tuscany, Italy
**Patronage:** Italy, Europe, nurses, fire prevention, people ridiculed for their piety, sick people
**Rarity:** Epic

## Description
Catherine of Siena was the twenty-fourth child in a family of twenty-five, a girl with no formal schooling who learned to read as a teenager on her own — and went on to become one of the most powerful voices in the history of the Church. She cared for plague victims no one else would touch, dictated mystical visions and brilliant theological letters while in states of ecstasy, received the invisible stigmata in her hands and feet, and — most astonishingly — personally wrote blunt, fearless letters to Pope Gregory XI telling him to stop being afraid and return the papacy from Avignon back to Rome. He actually did it. She was declared a Doctor of the Church, one of only four women ever given that title. For every young person who has ever been told they are too young, too unqualified, or too small to matter, Catherine is living proof that God uses exactly who He chooses.

## Character Attributes (Symbols)
- Stigmata glowing at the palms — she received the invisible stigmata from Christ and bore those wounds for the rest of her life; she requested they remain invisible so as not to attract attention, and God honored that request
- Small papal tiara at her feet — her extraordinary historical act of writing to Pope Gregory XI and persuading him to return from Avignon to Rome, restoring the papacy; she called him "our sweet Christ on earth" while telling him firmly he was wrong
- Burning heart at her chest — her core mystical experience: an exchange of hearts with Christ, described in her Dialogue; the fire of God's love that consumed and transformed her completely
