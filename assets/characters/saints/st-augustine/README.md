# St. Augustine of Hippo
**Category:** Saints
**Feast Day:** August 28
**Era:** 4th–5th Century (354–430 AD)
**Origin:** Thagaste, Numidia (modern Algeria); Bishop of Hippo (modern Algeria)
**Patronage:** Theologians, brewers, printers, sore eyes, scholars, the Order of Augustinians
**Rarity:** Epic

## Description
Augustine of Hippo spent his youth in brilliant, restless, spectacular failure — chasing pleasure, fame, and philosophical answers in every direction except the right one, living with a mistress for fifteen years, joining a Manichaean cult, moving to Rome and then Milan to advance his career as a rhetoric professor, all while his mother Monica prayed for him every night without stopping. Then one afternoon in a garden in Milan he heard a child's voice singing "take up and read," opened Paul's Letter to the Romans, and everything broke apart and reassembled around the truth. He became the greatest theologian the Western Church has ever produced, writing the Confessions — the first autobiography in Western literature and one of the most honest books ever written about what it feels like to be a human being looking for God — and the City of God, which shaped all of Western civilization. His story is for every young person who has gone looking for meaning in all the wrong places and still hasn't found what they're looking for.
