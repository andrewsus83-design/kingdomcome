# St. Teresa of Ávila
**Category:** Saints
**Feast Day:** October 15
**Era:** 16th Century (1515–1582)
**Origin:** Ávila, Castile, Spain
**Patronage:** Spain, headache sufferers, chess players, people in need of grace, Carmelites
**Rarity:** Epic

## Description
Teresa of Ávila was a sharp-witted, warm, sometimes irreverent Spanish noblewoman who entered the Carmelites at age twenty and spent nearly twenty years in comfortable, mediocre religious life before a second conversion experience lit her entirely on fire for God. She became the first woman ever declared a Doctor of the Church, wrote two of the greatest masterworks of Christian mysticism — The Interior Castle and The Way of Perfection — and simultaneously reformed the entire Carmelite Order across Spain while traveling on mule-carts through wind and mud and bureaucratic opposition in her sixties. She was so gifted in prayer that she literally levitated during Mass and had to grab onto a pillar to stay on the ground, and she famously complained to God: "If this is how you treat your friends, no wonder you have so few of them." Teresa shows every young person that prayer is not boring — it is the wildest, deepest, most surprising adventure the human soul can undertake.

## Character Attributes (Symbols)
- Interior Castle book and golden castle model — her mystical masterpiece describing the soul as a glittering castle with seven mansions leading to God at the center; she was the first woman declared Doctor of the Church
- Quill pen writing — she was a prolific and brilliant author who wrote while running a reform movement under Inquisition scrutiny; her writing was an act of obedience and spiritual warfare
- Angel with flaming spear — her famous transverberation vision: an angel pierced her heart with a golden flaming spear of divine love, leaving her in both pain and overwhelming joy; Bernini's famous sculpture depicts this moment
