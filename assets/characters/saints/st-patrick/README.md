# St. Patrick
**Category:** Saints
**Feast Day:** March 17
**Era:** 5th Century (~385–461 AD)
**Origin:** Roman Britain (modern Wales or Scotland), missionary to Ireland
**Patronage:** Ireland, Nigeria, engineers, excluded snakes, against fear
**Rarity:** Epic

## Description
Patrick was a sixteen-year-old Romano-British boy out walking near the coast when Irish pirates raided his village, seized him, and sold him into slavery in Ireland — where he spent six bitter years alone on cold hillsides tending sheep in all weather with almost no human contact. Rather than breaking him, those years made him one of the greatest prayer warriors who ever lived, and when he finally escaped back to Britain, God called him back in a dream — back to the very country that had enslaved him — to bring them the Gospel. He converted nearly the entire island of Ireland in his lifetime, famously using the three-leaf shamrock to explain the mystery of the Trinity, and drove out the snakes of paganism forever. His story of returning in love to the place of his greatest wound is one of the most astonishing acts of forgiveness in history.

## Character Attributes (Symbols)
- Glowing three-leaf shamrock — the tool he used to explain the Holy Trinity to the Irish: three leaves, one plant, one God in three Persons; now the national symbol of Ireland
- Golden Celtic knotwork vestments — the marriage of Irish culture and Christian faith that he forged; the endless interlocking knots represent eternity and the unbreakable love of God
- Serpents fleeing the cliff — the legendary driving of snakes from Ireland, understood spiritually as the expulsion of pagan darkness and evil; Patrick's power over the enemy
