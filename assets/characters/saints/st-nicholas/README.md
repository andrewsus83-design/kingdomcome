# St. Nicholas
**Category:** Saints
**Feast Day:** December 6
**Era:** 4th Century (270–343 AD)
**Origin:** Myra, Lycia (modern Turkey)
**Patronage:** Children, sailors, merchants, the falsely accused, pawnbrokers, Russia
**Rarity:** Rare

## Description
Nicholas of Myra was a bishop in ancient Turkey who was famous for his extraordinary generosity, especially toward children and the poor. The most famous story tells how he secretly tossed bags of gold through the window of a poor father's house at night so his daughters could afford to marry. He is the real historical figure behind the legend of Santa Claus — and the truth is even better than the story, because the real Nicholas gave everything not for fame, but purely for love of God and neighbor.

## Character Attributes (Symbols)
- Three golden balls — the bags of gold he tossed secretly into the poor family's window; also the origin of the pawnbroker symbol
- Bishop's vestments — he became bishop of Myra at a young age and led his flock with extraordinary holiness
- Children — he is the universal patron of children, protecting and providing for them
