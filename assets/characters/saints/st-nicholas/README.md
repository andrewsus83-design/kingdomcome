# St. Nicholas
**Category:** Saints
**Feast Day:** December 6
**Era:** 4th Century (~270–343 AD)
**Origin:** Myra, Lycia (modern Turkey)
**Patronage:** Children, sailors, merchants, the falsely accused, pawnbrokers, Russia, gift-givers
**Rarity:** Rare

## Description
Nicholas of Myra was a bishop in ancient Asia Minor who was so extraordinary in his generosity that stories of his miracles and secret gift-giving spread across the entire Roman world within his own lifetime. The most famous story tells how he saved three sisters from being sold into slavery by secretly tossing three bags of gold coins through their father's window in the dead of night, one for each daughter's dowry — never once seeking credit or recognition. He was imprisoned and tortured for his faith under Emperor Diocletian and refused to renounce Christ, and at the Council of Nicaea he reportedly punched the heretic Arius in the face for denying Christ's divinity — which means the real Santa Claus was both incredibly generous and took theology extremely seriously. Nicholas is the proof that true giving is anonymous, costly, and entirely for the other person's sake.

## Character Attributes (Symbols)
- Three golden balls or bags — the three bags of gold he threw secretly through the poor man's window to save his three daughters; also the historical origin of the three golden balls outside pawnbrokers' shops, and of leaving gifts secretly at night
- Deep crimson bishop's vestments — his office as Bishop of Myra, held for decades with extraordinary pastoral courage including imprisonment for his faith during the Diocletian persecution
- Delighted children at his knees — he is the universal patron and protector of children, saving them from harm by the power of prayer and generosity throughout his long ministry
