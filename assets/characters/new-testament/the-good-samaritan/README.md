# THE GOOD SAMARITAN
**Testament:** New Testament
**Book(s):** Luke 10:25–37
**Era:** 1st Century AD — Parable told by Jesus approximately 29–30 AD
**Role:** Parable of Compassion, Model of Neighbor-Love
**Rarity:** Rare

## Description
The Good Samaritan is the hero of one of Jesus's most famous and important parables, told in answer to the question "Who is my neighbor?" A man was brutally attacked on the dangerous road from Jerusalem to Jericho and left wounded and helpless in the dust, and two very religious men — a priest and a Levite — both saw him and deliberately crossed to the other side of the road to avoid getting involved. But a Samaritan traveler — someone from a group that Jewish people at the time looked down on and avoided — stopped everything, knelt in the road, bandaged the wounds with oil and wine from his own supplies, lifted the man onto his own donkey, rode with him to an inn, paid the innkeeper to look after him, and promised to pay any additional costs on his way back. Jesus ended the parable by asking, "Which of these three was a neighbor?" — and the answer teaches us that our neighbor is anyone who needs us, no matter who they are or where they come from.
