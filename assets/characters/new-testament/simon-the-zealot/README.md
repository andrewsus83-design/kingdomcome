# SIMON THE ZEALOT
**Testament:** New Testament
**Book(s):** Matthew 10, Mark 3, Luke 6, Acts 1
**Era:** 1st Century AD — approximately 27 AD onward
**Role:** Zealot Turned Peacemaker, Apostle
**Rarity:** Common

## Description
Simon the Zealot was one of Jesus's twelve apostles who got his nickname from the "Zealots" — a fierce and passionate Jewish movement that fought for freedom from Roman rule, willing to use force to achieve their goals. The remarkable thing about Jesus's choice of disciples is that in the same group He placed Simon the Zealot and Matthew the tax collector — two men who would normally have been sworn enemies, because tax collectors worked for the Romans that Zealots despised — and Jesus transformed them both into brothers. Simon's burning passion for justice was not extinguished by Jesus; it was redirected from fighting for a earthly kingdom to building an eternal one, and he carried the Gospel to Persia, Egypt, and beyond with the same unstoppable fire.
