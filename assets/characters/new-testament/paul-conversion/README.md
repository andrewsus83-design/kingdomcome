# PAUL (ROAD TO DAMASCUS)
**Testament:** New Testament
**Book(s):** Acts 9 & 22 & 26, Romans, 1 & 2 Corinthians, Galatians, Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 & 2 Thessalonians, 1 & 2 Timothy, Titus, Philemon
**Era:** 1st Century AD — conversion approximately 33–36 AD; ministry until approximately 67 AD
**Role:** Apostle to the Gentiles, Greatest Missionary of the Early Church
**Rarity:** Legendary

## Description
Before he became the greatest missionary in the history of the world, Paul — then called Saul of Tarsus — was actually the Church's most dangerous enemy, hunting down Christians across the region to have them arrested and imprisoned. Then came the Road to Damascus: a blinding explosion of heavenly light knocked him off his horse, Jesus spoke to him by name from the sky — "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me?" — and in that single moment, the most feared enemy of Christianity became its most unstoppable champion. Paul spent the next thirty years traveling more than ten thousand miles by land and sea, enduring shipwrecks, beatings, imprisonment, and every conceivable hardship to plant churches across the entire Roman Empire — and along the way he wrote thirteen letters that became books of the New Testament, giving us some of the most beautiful words ever written about love, faith, and the mercy of God.
